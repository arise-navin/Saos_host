CSDM KPI Articulation
Format per rule: mechanism, consequence, detection source, confidence basis, false-positive guard, remediation lane. Severity bands as before — Systemic above Critical.
One framing note before the catalogue. CSDM findings divide differently from the other domains. Most are structural absences rather than data defects, which means detection confidence is usually 100% (the record exists or it doesn't) while remediation confidence is low, because SAOS cannot invent service architecture. That asymmetry is why CSDM is the most Lane 3 heavy catalogue in the product, and saying so up front in a demo is what stops an architect assuming you're proposing to auto-generate their service model.
Group 1 — Taxonomy Conformance (CSDM-001 to 016)
CSDM-001 — Business Capability layer entirely unpopulated. Systemic. The top of the model doesn't exist, so nothing beneath it can roll up to business meaning. Every "business impact" figure the estate produces is asserted rather than derived. Detect: Zero records in the business capability class where the CSDM plugin is active. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Estate deliberately starting at Business Service — confirm the target CSDM band before raising as Systemic. Lane 3.
CSDM-002 — Business Capability with no Business Application beneath it. Critical. The capability is a label with nothing realising it. Capability-level reporting returns zero regardless of what runs underneath. Detect: Capabilities with no child application records. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Capabilities created ahead of a mapping programme. Lane 3.
CSDM-003 — Business Application with no Application Service. Critical. The application is modelled and has no runtime representation. Nothing connects it to infrastructure, so it can never appear in an impact result. Detect: Business applications with no related application service. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Applications recorded for portfolio purposes only, deliberately unmapped. Lane 3.
CSDM-004 — Business Service with no Service Offering. Critical. No commitment or SLA can attach. Incidents against the service are handled with no service-level target, and no consumer or channel differentiation exists in reporting. Detect: Business services with zero offering records as parent. Confidence: 100% on detection; the recommendation is evidence-based, not authoritative. Guard: Estate not using the offering layer at all — report as CSDM-062 instead. Lane 3, then Lane 1 for creation once decided.
CSDM-005 — Service Offering with no commitment or SLA. Critical. The offering exists to carry the commitment. Without one it adds a layer and no measurement. Detect: Offerings with no linked commitment and no SLA definition covering them. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Commitments held in a contract system with documented linkage. Lane 3. Joins PLT-028.
CSDM-006 — Application Service with no supporting CIs. Critical. The service layer terminates with nothing beneath it. Service maps are empty and impact analysis stops here. Detect: Application services with zero CI associations and zero supporting edges — check both mechanisms. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Externally hosted or SaaS applications. Lane 1 where mapping evidence supplies the CIs, Lane 3 otherwise.
CSDM-007 — Technical Service disconnected from any Application. High. The technical service describes a capability nothing consumes. Either the link is missing or the service is obsolete. Detect: Technical services with no relationship to an application or application service. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Infrastructure services consumed outside the modelled application estate. Lane 3.
CSDM-008 — Service classification inconsistent with the record's class. High. The classification field and the class disagree, so filters keyed on one return different populations than filters keyed on the other. Detect: service_classification values inconsistent with sys_class_name. Confidence: 100% on the comparison. Guard: Estate using classification for a purpose the class doesn't express. Lane 1 where unambiguous.
CSDM-009 — CI placed at the wrong CSDM layer. High. A server modelled as an application service, an application modelled as a business service. Every layer-scoped rule applies wrongly, and the hierarchy misrepresents what exists. Detect: Records whose attribute signature and relationship pattern match a different layer's profile by a margin. Confidence: 80% — layer inference from signature is the weakest CSDM rule; report with the evidence. Guard: Genuinely hybrid constructs. Lane 3.
CSDM-010 — Duplicate service constructs representing the same thing at two layers. Critical. "Payments" exists as a Business Service and as an Application Service with the same scope. Incidents split across both, and neither has a complete picture. Detect: Name and consumer overlap across layers, with overlapping supporting CI sets. Confidence: 85% — overlap is exact, the conclusion that it's unintended is inferred. Guard: Legitimate parent-child naming across layers. Lane 3.
CSDM-011 — Cross-layer shortcuts skipping a required layer. High. A Business Service related directly to a CI, bypassing offering and application service. Traversal works, and the intermediate layers are absent from the path, so offering-level and application-level reporting miss it. Detect: Edges whose endpoints span non-adjacent CSDM layers. Confidence: 95%. Guard: Deliberate simplified model — check whether shortcuts are the dominant pattern. Lane 3.
CSDM-012 — Service hierarchy depth below the estate's own standard. High. Derived from the best-modelled services rather than an imposed standard, so it measures internal consistency. Detect: Per-service depth against the depth achieved by the top decile of services. Confidence: 100% on measurement; expected depth is derived — state the derivation. Guard: Genuinely shallow services. Lane 3.
CSDM-013 — Business Application with no Business Capability parent. High. The application can't roll up to business meaning, so capability-level portfolio views exclude it. Detect: Applications with no capability relationship. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Capability layer not in use. Lane 3.
CSDM-014 — Information Object layer unpopulated where data classification is required. High. For a DPDP-regulated entity the information object layer is where data holdings attach to services. Its absence means no service can be traced to the data it processes. Detect: Zero or negligible information object records where personal data holdings exist. Confidence: 100% on population. Guard: Data classification maintained outside CSDM. Lane 3. Joins DQ-139.
CSDM-015 — Service records created outside the CSDM class tree. High. Custom classes or generic CIs used as services. They are invisible to every CSDM-scoped rule and to the service model as the platform understands it. Detect: Records referenced as services by incidents or SLAs whose class sits outside the CSDM tree. Confidence: 95%. Guard: Legacy service records retained for history. Lane 3.
CSDM-016 — Service portfolio count inconsistent with the business's own service catalogue. Moderate. The CMDB service list and the published catalogue describe different sets. Which one a consumer sees depends on where they look. Detect: Set comparison between CSDM service records and published catalogue entries. Confidence: 90% — matching relies on naming. Guard: Catalogue deliberately a subset. Lane 3.
Group 2 — Lifecycle Integrity (CSDM-017 to 032)
CSDM-017 — Lifecycle stage empty. Critical. Service portfolio reporting cannot distinguish live from planned from retired. Every portfolio figure is computed over an undifferentiated set. Detect: Service records with empty life_cycle_stage. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Estate not using the lifecycle model — report as CSDM-032. Lane 1 where inferable from corroborating signals, Lane 3 otherwise.
CSDM-018 — Lifecycle status empty while stage is populated. Critical. Stage without status is half the model. The permitted-combination validation can't apply, and reporting on status returns nothing. Detect: Populated stage with empty life_cycle_stage_status. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Status genuinely optional in the configured model. Lane 1.
CSDM-019 — Stage and status combination not in the permitted set. Critical. The combination is invalid against the estate's own configured model — read the instance's permitted set, never a hardcoded assumption. Detect: Pairs absent from the configured combination table. Confidence: 100% against the instance's own set. Guard: Customer has extended the model and the permitted set isn't documented — read it, don't assume it. Lane 1 for the bulk, Lane 3 where evidence contradicts.
CSDM-020 — Lifecycle contradicting operational_status. Critical. A service Operational in one field and Retired in another. Consumers read different fields and get different answers. Detect: Lifecycle stage inconsistent with operational_status per the mapping. Confidence: 95% — mapping-dependent. Guard: Transitional state during a planned retirement. Lane 3.
CSDM-021 — Service Operational whose supporting CIs are all Retired. Critical. The service claims to be live and everything beneath it is decommissioned. One of the two is wrong and both are consequential. Detect: Operational services where all supporting CIs are in a retired state. Confidence: 100% on the join. Guard: Migration in progress with new CIs not yet associated. Lane 3.
CSDM-022 — Service Retired still consumed by open incidents. Critical. Support is being provided for a service the business believes is gone. Detect: Retired-stage services with open incident references. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Incidents raised to complete the retirement. Lane 3.
CSDM-023 — Service in End of Life with active SLAs. Critical. A commitment is being enforced on a service scheduled for removal. Either the retirement is wrong or the commitment should have been withdrawn. Detect: End-of-life stage with active SLA definitions covering the service. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Wind-down period with contractual obligations continuing. Lane 3.
CSDM-024 — End of Life with live supporting CIs and recent incident volume. Critical, low remediation confidence. The evidence genuinely contradicts itself — this is the population you hold back from bulk remediation. In the earlier worked example these were the 9 records at 54% confidence, deliberately excluded from the Fix All. Detect: End-of-life services with operational CIs and incidents in the last 90 days. Confidence: Detection 100%; remediation confidence explicitly low — do not bulk-apply. Guard: This finding is the ambiguity. Present both interpretations. Lane 3.
CSDM-025 — Lifecycle never updated since creation. Moderate. Suggests lifecycle isn't managed for that class at all. Weak per record, strong as a class-level ratio. Detect: No lifecycle field audit entry, age above 365 days. Confidence: 100% on audit absence — report as a pattern, not individual findings. Guard: Genuinely stable services. Lane 3.
CSDM-026 — Entire class sitting at the default lifecycle value. Systemic. When every record in a class holds the default, nobody ever adopted the model. This is a stronger and more honest finding than the thousands of individual empty-field findings it replaces. Detect: Class-level distribution where one value exceeds 90% and equals the platform default. Confidence: 100% on the distribution. Guard: Default genuinely correct for most records — check whether any record has ever deviated. Lane 3.
CSDM-027 — Lifecycle transitions occurring with no corresponding change record. High. Services are being retired and activated with no change control. For a regulated entity this is a control finding. Detect: Lifecycle audit entries with no change record in the correlation window. Confidence: 90%. Guard: Bulk lifecycle correction under a documented plan. Lane 3. Joins ITSM-106.
CSDM-028 — Service lifecycle inconsistent with its parent's lifecycle. High. An Operational application service under a Retired business service. The hierarchy contradicts itself vertically. Detect: Parent-child lifecycle pairs that are logically incompatible. Confidence: 90% — incompatibility matrix dependent. Guard: Staged retirement working bottom-up. Lane 3.
CSDM-029 — Service in a planning stage with production incidents. Critical. The service is live and the model says it isn't. Portfolio reporting understates the operational estate. Detect: Pipeline or planning stage with production incident volume. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Incidents raised during pilot. Lane 1 where evidence is unambiguous.
CSDM-030 — Lifecycle stage populated with a free-text or non-choice value. High. Arrived via import bypassing validation. Reporting grouped on stage splits across phantom values. Detect: Stage values absent from the configured choice set. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Recently retired choice held by historical records. Lane 1 for mapping, Lane 2 for the validation gap.
CSDM-031 — No validation preventing invalid lifecycle combinations. Systemic. This is the prevention finding that must accompany any lifecycle remediation. Fixing 134 records without fixing the gate means they return within six months, and the drift agent will catch it. Detect: No data policy or UI policy enforcing the permitted combination set. Confidence: 100% on configuration absence. Guard: Enforcement through a business rule the scan didn't classify. Lane 2.
CSDM-032 — Lifecycle model not configured at all. Systemic. No permitted combination set, no stage choices beyond defaults. There is nothing to validate against, so every lifecycle finding below this one is unmeasurable. Detect: Absence of configured stage/status reference data. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Model held externally. Lane 2.
Group 3 — Ownership (CSDM-033 to 042)
CSDM-033 — Service has no owner. High. Nobody is accountable for the service. Incidents escalate to a default, changes have no owner approval, and no attestation can be assigned. Detect: Services with empty owned_by and managed_by. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Ownership at the offering level. Lane 1 where inferable from incident or change history, Lane 3 otherwise.
CSDM-034 — Service owner is an inactive user. Critical. The field is populated so every completeness metric passes. Escalations route to somebody who has left. Detect: Owner references resolving to active=false. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Grace period with a documented successor. Lane 1 where a successor is inferable, Lane 3 otherwise. Joins DQ-095.
CSDM-035 — Support group has zero active members. Critical. Nominal ownership complete, effective ownership absent. Report both numbers — the gap is usually large and it's the finding that reframes their ownership dashboard. Detect: Support group resolving to zero active members at evaluation time. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Group in transition with reassignment in progress. Lane 3. Joins CMDB-102, ITSM-134.
CSDM-036 — Business Service with no business relationship manager. High. No business-side counterpart. Service reviews, demand and commitment negotiation have no named participant from the consuming side. Detect: Business services with empty BRM field. Confidence: 100%. Guard: Role not used in this organisation. Lane 3.
CSDM-037 — Ownership inconsistent between a Business Application and its Application Services. High. Escalation paths diverge. During an incident two groups believe they own the response, or neither does. Detect: Owner or support group differing between parent and child. Confidence: 80% — divergence is a signal, not proof of error. Guard: Deliberate separation of portfolio and operational ownership. Lane 3.
CSDM-038 — Single owner holding a disproportionate share of the service portfolio. Moderate. Usually bulk assignment during migration rather than real ownership. Creates a single point of failure in the accountability model. Detect: Owners holding more than 20% of services. Confidence: 100% on distribution; the judgement is advisory. Guard: Genuinely centralised service management function. Lane 3.
CSDM-039 — Service ownership never changed since creation despite organisational change. Moderate. Set once at migration and never maintained. Report as a class ratio. Detect: No ownership audit entry beyond 365 days. Confidence: 100% on audit absence. Guard: Stable ownership is legitimate. Lane 3.
CSDM-040 — Service with no data owner where it processes personal data. Critical. DPDP requires an accountable party for data processing. A service handling personal data with no data owner is a governance gap with regulatory consequence. Detect: Services linked to personal-data information objects or tables, with no data owner assigned. Confidence: 100% on assignment; the personal-data linkage confidence depends on classification quality. Guard: Data ownership held in an external register. Lane 3. Feeds the Regulatory Evidence Pack.
CSDM-041 — Offering owner differs from parent service owner with no documented rationale. Moderate. Offerings inherit accountability from the service unless deliberately separated. Undocumented divergence creates ambiguity at escalation. Detect: Offering owner differing from parent service owner. Confidence: 100% on the comparison. Guard: Deliberate offering-level ownership model. Lane 3.
CSDM-042 — No service ownership review or attestation process evidenced. Systemic. Ownership is assigned once and never revalidated. Every ownership finding above accumulates because nothing removes stale assignments. Detect: No attestation or review records covering service ownership within the review period. Confidence: 100% on record absence. Guard: Review conducted externally with evidence. Lane 3.

Change risk, SLA applicability and impact calculation all treat them identically. Detect: Application services with empty environment field. Confidence: 100%. CSDM-045 — Non-production services carrying production SLAs. Critical. A commitment is being measured against a test environment. Breach figures include work nobody committed to. CSDM-046 — Business Service supported only by non-production application services. Critical. The business service has no production realisation. CSDM-047 — Production service supported by non-production CIs. Critical. Production depends on infrastructure tagged for test. CSDM-048 — Environment values inconsistent between a service and its CIs. High. The service says production, the CIs say staging. Environment-scoped rules apply to one and not the other. CSDM-049 — Non-production services counted in production service portfolio reporting. High. The portfolio overstates the live estate. Capacity, cost and criticality figures all inflate. CSDM-050 — No environment value set defined or enforced. Systemic. There's nothing to populate and nothing to validate against. Every environment finding above is unmeasurable until this is fixed. Two notes for the build
Gating matters more in CSDM than anywhere else. Several rules are unmeasurable until a prerequisite is fixed: CSDM-032 gates the entire lifecycle group, CSDM-050 gates environment, CSDM-062 gates the offering group, and CSDM-043 gates CSDM-044 through 046. Firing the dependent rules before the gate is resolved produces thousands of findings with one cause, which is the exact noise the Root Cause Agent exists to prevent. Encode the gating in the rule pack, not in the report.
The prevention findings carry the subscription. CSDM-031 (no lifecycle validation), CSDM-042 (no ownership review), CSDM-050 (no environment enforcement) and CSDM-074 (non-conformant creation continuing) are what stop the remediated estate from degrading back. Fixing 143 lifecycle values is a service. Fixing the reason they went wrong, and proving six months later that they stayed right, is the product.
